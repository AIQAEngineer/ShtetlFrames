            In the center of the city — and yet far from its noise — stands the old Jewish cemetery. In the 17th century burial here ceased, and the peace of the dead was undisturbed until 1903, when by decision of the Prague municipality the cemetery boundary had to be moved by the full width of today’s Sanytrová Street, where the Czech Academic Gymnasium now stands. Two thousand tombstones were cleared away, and several thousand of the dead were reburied on Nefele Hill, where in 1906 the burial society erected a monument.

V 17. století se přestává zde pochovávat a klid mrtvých není rušen až v roce 1903, kdy rozhodnutím pražské obce musí být hranice hřbitova posunuta o celou šíři dnešní ulice Sanytrové, kde stojí nyní české akademické gymnasium. Dva tisíce náhrobních kamenů odklizeno a několik tisíc mrtvých pohřbeno znovu na návrší Nefele, kdež byl v roce 1906 pohřebním bratrstvem postaven pomník.

                    English
                    In the center of the city — and yet far from its noise — stands the old Jewish cemetery. In the 17th century burial here ceased, and the peace of the dead was undisturbed until 1903, when by decision of the Prague municipality the cemetery boundary had to be moved by the full width of today’s Sanytrová Street, where the Czech Academic Gymnasium now stands. Two thousand tombstones were cleared away, and several thousand of the dead were reburied on Nefele Hill, where in 1906 the burial society erected a monument.

                    1:53–2:21 — The tomb of Mordecai Meisel
                    Czech
                    — hrobky Mordechaje Meisela — syna Samuelova — který zemřel v roce 106 (hebr.). Tento velký dobrodinec vystavěl svým nákladem tak zvanou Vysokou synagogu i synagogu, jež má po něm jméno, dal zříditi lázně pro ženy, chudobinec, nemocnici a na svůj náklad dal také vydlážditi celé židovské město. Císařem byl mu propůjčen titul vládního rady.

                    English
                    — the tomb of Mordecai Meisel — son of Samuel — who died in the year 106 (Hebrew). This great benefactor built at his own expense the so-called High Synagogue and the synagogue that bears his name; he had baths for women, an almshouse, and a hospital established, and at his own expense also had the entire Jewish quarter paved. The Emperor conferred on him the title of Imperial Councillor.

                    3:04–3:27 — The tomb of Rabbi Löw
                    Czech
                    — kde spatřujeme náhrobek muže nejznámějšího z historie, rabbiho Jehudy, syna Besalelova (Rabbi Löw). Zemřel v roce 1609 (hebr.). Pověst o něm říká, že stvořil z hlíny člověka, jemuž dal jméno Golem. Ve válečných letech chodili k jeho hrobu polští uprchlíci a skulinou do náhrobku vkládali žádosti a prosby, aby všemocný rabbi jejich utrpení zmírnil.

                    English
                    — where we see the tombstone of the most famous man in history, Rabbi Judah, son of Bezalel (Rabbi Löw). He died in the year 1609 (Hebrew). Legend says of him that he created from clay a man whom he named Golem. In the war years, Polish refugees would come to his grave and through a crack in the tombstone place requests and prayers that the all-powerful rabbi might ease their suffering.

                    4:05–4:28 — The tomb of Hendel bas Shava
                    Czech
                    Umělecký náhrobek, pod nímž je pochována v roce 1628 Hendel bas Šava, šlechtična z Treuenbergu. Její choť, Jakub Smiles, měl právo ražební a pro velké zásluhy o stát byla mu císařem Rudolfem II. propůjčena výsadní listina, kteráž byla císařem Matyášem znovu potvrzena. Císař Ferdinand II. jej pak povýšil do stavu šlechtického.

                    English
                    An artistic tombstone, under which was buried in 1628 Hendel bas Shava, a noblewoman of Treuenberg. Her husband, Jakob Smiles, held the right of coinage, and for great services to the state Emperor Rudolf II granted him a privilege charter, which was again confirmed by Emperor Matthias. Emperor Ferdinand II then raised him to the nobility.
                    4:44–4:55 — Rabbi David Oppenheimer
                    Czech
                    Velký učenec rabbi David Oppenheimer, syn Abrahámův, zemřel v roce 1736. V hodnosti zemského rabína země české potvrzen byl císaři: Leopoldem I., Josefem I. i Karlem VI. Jeho velikou knihovnu zakoupila universita oxfordská.

                    English
                    The great scholar Rabbi David Oppenheimer, son of Abraham, died in the year 1736. In the office of Land Rabbi of the Czech lands he was confirmed by the emperors: Leopold I, Joseph I, and Charles VI. His great library was purchased by Oxford University.

                    English
                    — and as we leave we are directed to a tombstone on which a monk is carved in the center of the slab, with two roosters around him. Legend says that a certain Jew, having had himself baptized, entered a monastery. His last wish before death was to be buried in the Jewish cemetery.
