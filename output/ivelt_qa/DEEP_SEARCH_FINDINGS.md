## 1. Prague Old Jewish Cemetery film (1926) — t=83175

### What the post said

### Archive facts
| | |
|--|--|
| Czech title | *Židovský hřbitov na Starém městě pražském* |
| English (EFG) | *The Jewish Cemetery in the Staré město pražské (The Old Prague City)* |
| Latin on card | *Antiquitates Judaicae Pragenses* |
| Holder | Czech National Film Archive (NFA) |
| EFG id | `nfa::55d45b72c1c66f42b896a1eeb67cdfdc` |
| Stream | https://data.nfa.cz/video/mp4/efg/film/0063535.mp4 |
| In project | `output/efg_discovery.csv` |

### Historical background (what the film is showing)
The Old Jewish Cemetery (Josefov) was Prague’s main Jewish burial ground from the early 1400s until **1787**, when Joseph II banned inner-city burials. Space was so tight that graves were stacked many layers deep; ~12,000 stones are visible today, far fewer than the number of people buried.

**People named on the intertitles (all real graves there):**
- **Mordecai Meisel** (d. 1601) — court Jew / philanthropist; built Meisel Synagogue, High Synagogue, paved the ghetto, baths, hospital.
- **Rabbi Judah Loew (Maharal / Rabbi Löw)** (d. 1609) — Prague’s most famous rabbi; Golem legend; Polish refugees in wartime stuffed notes in a crack in his stone (film mentions this).
- **Hendel bas Shava / Treuenberg** (d. 1628) — noblewoman; husband Jakob Smiles had minting privileges under Rudolf II.
- **Rabbi David Oppenheim (Oppenheimer)** (1664–1736) — Land Rabbi of Bohemia. His huge Hebrew library (~7,000 prints + ~1,000 MSS) was kept in Hanover to escape Prague censorship; Bodleian Library, Oxford bought it in **1829**. The film’s line “his great library was purchased by Oxford University” is accurate.

**1903 episode the film narrates:** During Josefov “sanitization” (clearance/rebuild), the city forced a road through the cemetery edge — Sanytrová, today’s **17. listopadu**. ~2,000 stones cleared; remains reburied on **Nefele** (Hebrew *nefel* = miscarriage) mound by the Klausen Synagogue — originally the infant burial hill. Chevra Kadisha put up a monument in **1906**. The 1926 film is essentially a guided tour of that recent wound plus the famous tombs, ~20 years later.

### Postcard album connection
Same Latin title appears on a ~**1910–1920s** souvenir booklet: **25 sepia cards** of Altneuschul + Old Cemetery graves, printed by **M. Schulz, Prague**, for the burial society (Chevra Kadisha / Gomel Hasidim), cord-bound with a red Star-of-David seal. Held by YIVO, Jewish Museum Berlin (Inv. 2017/312/628), dealers. Photos: `output/ivelt_qa/antiquitates_postcards/`.  
So the film borrows the same “Jewish antiquities of Prague” branding language as a popular tourist souvenir of the same decade — not that the postcard set *is* the film.

### What’s missing online
Almost no English article discusses this **film** as a film. Cemetery history is everywhere; the reel itself is obscure outside Czech NFA / EFG.

## 2. 1,650 Chachmei Lublin stamped seforim — t=83177

### What the post said
1,650 seforim with Yeshivas Chachmei Lublin stamps identified, scanned, online at yeshivalibrary.org.

### Project & people
- **Piotr Nazaruk** + **Monika Tarajko** (Grodzka Gate – Teatr NN, Lublin), with ŻIH library staff and others.
- Site: https://yeshivalibrary.org/ — data from Google Sheet `1N--MCZSwjbLchUT37i-Ph5TwHoMxqNXUsO_zPngtokg`.
- **Live count checked: exactly 1,650 rows** (matches the post).

### Why it matters
Yeshivas Chachmei Lublin was founded by **Rav Meir Shapiro** (opened 1930; cornerstone celebrated Lag BaOmer 1924). He wanted a modern yeshiva *and* one of Poland’s greatest Torah libraries. Donations poured in — e.g. Rabbi Benjamin Gut (Chasam Sopher, NY) sent ~**4,000** books + $1,000. After Shapiro’s death (1933), his private collection was absorbed. Pre-war estimates: **~15,000–40,000** volumes (he once spoke of aiming toward 100,000).

**The burned-library legend:** Popular story said Nazis burned the books in the courtyard with a band playing while a Jewish man wept. Nazaruk showed 1941/1944 sources that the library still existed; books were **catalogued, crated, looted, and scattered**. Rav Aharon Lebwohl was forced to catalogue crates (1941–murdered Majdanek 1942); his catalogue is lost. Postwar, many volumes surfaced in ŻIH Warsaw, NLI, auctions, yeshivas, private hands.

### Growth of the digital catalogue
| When | Known stamped volumes |
|------|------------------------|
| Few years ago | ~5 |
| 2020 (ŻIH dig) | 130 |
| Dec 2023 | 850 |
| Dec 2024 (2nd annual report) | **1,400** |
| Now (live sheet) | **1,650** |

2024 jump mainly from a multi-day ŻIH stack search (~400 finds). Books have also been ceremonially returned to Lublin (e.g. via Meir Bulka / Israeli school groups; Freie Universität Berlin; Genazym). Covered by JTA, Times of Israel, Haaretz, Mishpacha, Behadrei, Pakn Treger.

**Bottom line:** Your number is not a guess — it’s the current online catalogue size, and still only a small slice of the pre-war library.

## 4. Jerusalem — 1964 (Pathé 86316)

### What the post said
ירושלים — 1964 + Pathé link.

### What Pathé actually filed
- Title: ***Selected Originals - THE PAPAL PILGRIMAGE*** (1964)
- Outtakes/rushes for newsreel **“The Papal Pilgrimage” 64/6**
- https://www.britishpathe.com/asset/86316/
- Description includes Technicolor cuts of Pope **Paul VI**’s Holy Land trip, holy sites, frontiers, Galilee, Bethlehem, Jordan/Israel — and explicitly **“Orthodox Jewish boys and men.”**

### Event context
**4–6 January 1964:** first modern pope in the Holy Land; Jerusalem still **divided** (Israel / Jordan). Huge chaotic crowds at Damascus Gate and Via Dolorosa on the Jordanian side; historic meetings with Patriarch **Athenagoras** on the Mount of Olives (first pope–patriarch encounter since 1438). Israel side was more orderly; Chief Rabbi Nissim declined formal greeting unless the Pope visited the Rabbinate; other rabbis and Religious Affairs Minister Warhaftig did appear at state ceremonies.

**So your post:** You’re pointing at the **Orthodox Jewish cutaways** inside leftover papal-pilgrimage footage — not a dedicated “Jewish Jerusalem” newsreel title.

## 5. Davening at artillery, Eilat — 1957 (Pathé 206920)

### What the post said
מתפלל ביי אן ארטילעריע פאזיציע אין אילת — 1957

### What Pathé actually filed
- Title: ***ISRAEL NAVY WARSHIPS ARRIVE IN EILAT BASE ENDING RED SEA BLOCKAGE*** (1957)
- Canister **UN 2923 E** · ~4:09 · https://www.britishpathe.com/asset/206920/
- Shot list: Red Sea / Sinai aerials; **soldiers & civilians around a large field gun**; artillery aiming to sea; spiked barrel; gun firing; dug-out; destroyers in quay; AA practice; semaphore; evening binoculars; “man in foreground in **arab dress**” at the gun position.

### Event context
After the **1956 Suez/Sinai war**, Egypt’s blockade of the **Straits of Tiran** (gateway to Eilat) was broken. Israel withdrew from Sharm el-Sheikh under UN pressure only after insisting on free passage; **UNEF** deployed. Eilat’s port had been built mid-1950s and opened for cargo in **1957** as Israel’s Red Sea door to Asia/Africa. Sister Pathé reel: **206942** *First Israeli ships at Eilat base*. Period also saw Saudi–Israeli gunfire incidents in the Gulf of Aqaba (Jan–May 1957).

**So your post:** Same navy/Eilat story reel; the artillery position is catalogued. Pathé does **not** label the foreground man as davening — that is a visual read of the frame. Historically the guns matter: they were how access to Eilat was fought over and then guarded.

## 7. Cards at Brooklyn Jewish old-age home — 1932 (Pathé 64645)

### What the post said
שפילן קארטן אינעם ברוקלינער אידישן מושב זקנים — 1932

### What Pathé actually filed
- Title: ***THE SUPER FORCING SYSTEM*** · Issue date **26 January 1932** · ~1:26
- https://www.britishpathe.com/asset/64645/
- Full titles joke that, “fired by all this talk about the **B.B.B. (Big Bridge Battles)**,” inmates of **“New York’s Hebrew Home for Aged”** decide to play… Officials ask what system they use; deal; women knit; comic argument over calling someone a “dummy.”

### Why “forcing system” / Jan 1932
Peak of the **Culbertson vs Lenz “Bridge Battle of the Century”** (Dec 1931–Jan 1932, NYC hotels) — front-page publicity for Ely Culbertson’s **approach-forcing** contract bridge system. Pathé’s gag is riding that news cycle: even the zekeinim at the Hebrew Home are arguing systems.

### Institution
Pathé only says “New York.” Strong candidate for your Brooklyn label: **Brooklyn Hebrew Home and Hospital for the Aged**, Howard Ave & Dumont (Brownsville) — founded as Brooklyn Ladies Hebrew Home for the Aged (~1907), renamed through 1910s, kosher/religious home + hospital, ~450 beds by early 1920s expansions. Active through the 1930s (BPL has 1939 Rosh Hashana shofar photo of residents). Moved partly to Coney Island from 1953; Brownsville buildings razed mid-1970s. (There were other “Hebrew Home” institutions in the metro area, so borough isn’t 100% proven from Pathé text alone.)

## Round 2 — new online finds (fresh search)

### Prague 1926
- Easy to confuse with other films titled *Židovský hřbitov*: Tomáš Mazal’s **1984** short (Žižkov cemetery + Egon Bondy) and Loznitsa’s Riga cemetery piece are **different**. The 1926 NFA/EFG Josefov reel remains almost unmentioned in Czech film databases beyond the archive gateway.
- No new filmmaker credit or contemporary 1926 premiere notice turned up.

### Lublin 1,650
- Teatr NN page **“Powroty książek”** (Book Returns): between **2022–2025**, **over a dozen** physical volumes returned to Lublin from Freie Universität Berlin (Stefan Kummer), Meir Bulka, Tzvi Grossman, **Genazym** (Chaim Stefansky), **Bezeq**, and Israeli school groups (Ulpenat Tveria, Givat-Shmuel, Modi’in, Amit Modi’in).
- Separate thread: looted yeshiva **correspondence** returned April 2024 by Karla McCabe (granddaughter of a Wehrmacht soldier).
- No newer formal annual report than Dec 2024’s 1,400; live sheet at 1,650 is still the freshest public count.

### Pathé 259561 / Kaufman
- **New family trail:** *J. Weekly* obituary (2008) for **Bernie Kaufman Jr.** (1914–2008) — son who was on the 1935 trip as a young man. Family lived in **Vienna** in the 1920s (father studied medicine there; Bernie met Freud as a child); returned SF 1928; Bernie later liberated **Buchenwald** as a US Army doctor and helped smuggle refugees toward Palestine. Self-published ***Tales of the Kaufman Family*** (2004) — best next place to hunt a Europe stop-list for the film.
- Daughter **Deborah Kaufman** founded the SF Jewish Film Festival; Magnes holds related family papers (Edith Schoenberger Kaufman, Sr.’s later wife).
- Still no Pathé/Filmkontor donor ledger online proving accession of 259561.

### Eilat 1957
- Parallel visual culture: Getty/AFP still of an IDF soldier in **tallit + tefillin** praying in Sinai, released **5 Nov 1956** (Suez) — same war cycle as the Eilat navy/guns story. Supports why a “davening at a military position” reading feels period-typical even if Pathé’s shot card only says “arab dress.”
- JFC has related navy ceremonials (e.g. INS *Yaffo* / INS *Eilat* arrival from British shipyards — different event; don’t conflate with Pathé 206920).

### Jerusalem 1964 / 1967
- No new Pathé catalog corrections found; prior readings (papal outtakes; Morocco can with Jerusalem cuts) still stand.

### Brooklyn / Hebrew Home 1932
- Still no online source naming the Pathé set as Brooklyn specifically.
- Competing NY institutions also used “Hebrew Home for the Aged” branding (e.g. Harlem→Riverdale line founded 1917; Home of the Daughters of Jacob earlier). Pathé’s wording stays ambiguous; Brooklyn remains plausible, not proven.

### Rosh Hashana 1922
- Still no titled newsreel match (Fox/Hearst/Kinogram/Pathé News). NFPF has miscellaneous Pathé News 1922 issues, not RH-specific. Place remains unknown.
