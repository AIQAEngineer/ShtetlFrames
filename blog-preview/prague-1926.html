1926 · Prague · Czech National Film Archive

A silent film of Prague’s Old Jewish Cemetery

A hundred-year-old Czech short of Josefov’s cemetery — with every on-screen title translated into English. Almost nobody writes about it online.

Archive title: Židovský hřbitov na Starém městě pražském · Antiquitates Judaicae Pragenses

In 1926 the Czech film archive recorded a short documentary of the Old Jewish Cemetery in Prague’s Old Town — the layered stones of Josefov, the Old-New Synagogue beside them, and the graves of figures still spoken about today: Mordecai Meisel, Rabbi Judah Löw, David Oppenheimer.

The English-speaking web barely mentions this reel. Tour pages describe the cemetery. A later PBS documentary tells its stories. But this silent Czech original sits mainly in archive catalogs — European Film Gateway, the Czech National Film Archive — without a public English write-up of what the title cards actually say.

What the film shows

It opens on the Old-New Synagogue, then walks the overgrown paths of the cemetery. Intertitles name the stones: Meisel the benefactor; Rabbi Löw and the Golem legend; Polish refugees slipping notes into a crack in his tomb; Oppenheimer, whose library went to Oxford.

One card recalls the 1903 cut to the cemetery edge for Sanytrová Street — two thousand stones moved, thousands reburied on Nefele Hill.

Why this matters

English search for “Jewish cemetery Prague 1926” turns up a WWI memorial at a different cemetery — not this film. The reel is catalogued; it isn’t discussed. Putting the titles into English is a small way to make a silent archive speak again.
